I am a political scientist at the **Centre for Democracy Studies Aarau** at the **University of Zurich** working on democratic governance, political behaviour, digital democracy and democratic innovation.

My research focuses particularly on **deliberative democracy**, **political participation**, **political behaviour**, and the emerging relationship between **artificial intelligence and democracy**. A central theme of my work is how democratic institutions can improve citizens' reasoning, inclusion, and participation, and how new technologies can support—or undermine—these processes.

## Research

My current research is organised around four connected areas:

- **Artificial intelligence and democracy** — the opportunities and risks created by LLMs and AI-mediated deliberation, including AI facilitation, multi-agent deliberation, and human–AI democratic systems.
- **Deliberative democracy and democratic innovations** — how citizens deliberate, reason, and form political judgements in minipublics and other participatory institutions.
- **Political behaviour and participation** — how citizens engage with democratic institutions and how political attitudes and preferences develop.
- **Democratic inclusion and representation** — how democratic processes represent different perspectives, discourses, and forms of political reasoning.

My work combines experimental and observational quantitative methods with comparative research and set-theoretic approaches.

## Current work

I am currently involved in research on:

- AI-supported and AI-mediated democratic deliberation
- the design and evaluation of citizens' assemblies and minipublics
- deliberative reasoning and discourse representation
- political participation and democratic resilience
- methods for benchmarking LLM reasoning against human judgement
