My research is developed through collaborative and externally funded projects spanning democratic innovation, political behaviour, collective reasoning, and artificial intelligence.

## Ongoing Projects

### Human–Machine Interaction for Democracy (HMI4D)
**2026–2028**  
**Role:** Co-Investigator  
**Funding:** Hasler Foundation & Mercator Foundation  
**Total funding:** CHF 591,000  

HMI4D investigates how interactions between humans and artificial intelligence can be designed to support democratic communication and collective reasoning. The project examines the opportunities and risks of integrating AI systems into deliberative and participatory democratic processes.

---

### AI4DELIBERATION
**2024–2027**  
**Role:** Work Package Leader for the Swiss contribution  
**Funding:** Horizon Europe — CL2-2024-DEMOCRACY-01-07  
**Consortium funding:** approximately €3 million  
**Swiss component:** approximately €407,000  

AI4DELIBERATION examines how artificial intelligence can support large-scale, digitally mediated democratic deliberation. My work focuses particularly on the design and evaluation of AI-supported deliberative processes and the integration of AI into institutionalised democratic participation.

---

### Swiss Citizens’ Assembly
**2022–2026**  
**Role:** Senior Research Fellow / Researcher  
**Funding:** Swiss National Science Foundation (SNSF) — BRIDGE  
**Total funding:** CHF 1.2 million  

The project studies a national-level citizens’ assembly as a democratic innovation combining sortition, deliberation, and participatory democracy. My research focuses on deliberative reasoning, political judgement, institutional design, and the effects of minipublic participation.

The project is conducted with researchers at the University of Zurich and the University of Geneva.

## Selected Completed Projects

### Triage Against the Machine: Can AI Reason Deliberatively?
**2025**  
**Role:** Principal Investigator  
**Funding:** Swiss National Science Foundation (SNSF) — SPARK Grant  
**Funding:** CHF 94,100  

This project investigated whether large language models can reproduce forms of deliberative reasoning observed among humans.

It develops a human-grounded approach to evaluating AI reasoning by benchmarking LLM outputs against empirical evidence from human deliberation.

### Australian Citizens’ Jury on Genome Editing
**2020–2023**  
**Role:** Collaborator  
**Funding:** Australian Research Council (ARC) — LP190101198
